## Definition
To explain the eight vertex model, we must first understand the vertex model.

The vertex model is a lattice model in which the microstates of a system are represented by looking at the edges between lattice sites (the nearest neighbours interactions for example). The eight vertex model is a generalization of this model for the square lattice that employs the following rule - the number of arrows pointing in and out of a vertex site must be even. The importance of this model is it's universality class. A universality class of a model is defined by it's critical behaviour - specifically, it's invariant critical behaviour under renormalization (scale invariant). One way of looking at this is at a large enough scale (limit), the models all look the same.
